\noindent Dear Editor,

\medskip
\noindent We submit for your consideration the manuscript entitled
``Persistent Negative Lag-One Dependence in Locally Detrended
Prime-Gap Windows up to $N = 10^9$, with Absence of Significant
Positive Autocorrelation up to $K = 1024$'' by BERRAMDANE Reddouane.

\medskip
\noindent \textbf{Summary of the contribution.} We analyse the
autocorrelation of consecutive prime gaps using an exact segmented
sieve up to $N = 10^9$ (50{,}847{,}532 gaps). The lag-one
autocorrelation of the locally detrended gap sequence is negative in
all ten logarithmic windows spanning $[10^4, 10^9)$, with moving-block
bootstrap intervals excluding zero, and its magnitude follows a power
law in $\log p$ with exponent $c = 1.28 \pm 0.05$. Extending the
analysis to $K = 1024$ lags on the globally detrended sequence, we find
no significant positive autocorrelation at any individual lag. We also
report a direct test of the Lemke--Oliver--Soundararajan conjecture
mod~3, and an inclusion--exclusion extension of the naive
Hardy--Littlewood pair-density heuristic that reduces the mean absolute
error on observed pair ratios from $3.55\%$ to $0.37\%$ with no fitted
constants.

\medskip
\noindent \textbf{Negative autocorrelation and prime gaps.} Negative
correlations between consecutive gaps are classical, going back to
Brent (1974), and the existence of long gaps is well established
(Ford, Green, Konyagin, Maynard and Tao, 2018). The literature treats
these mostly as separate phenomena. Our contribution is to measure the
correlation itself, with a windowed and detrended estimator, to
$10^9$, and to quantify its decay exponent --- with an explicit
synthetic control (Appendix C) showing the negative sign is not an
artifact of the detrending procedure.

\medskip
\noindent \textbf{Where the methodology may interest the readership.}
Three features of the analysis are transferable beyond number theory.
First, the raw global correlogram of prime gaps exhibits a strong
positive plateau that is purely an artifact of non-stationarity; we
demonstrate this with a synthetic control (Appendix B). Second, we
show that the common argument ``the white-noise standard error is
conservative here'' is not generally sound, and give the correct
Bartlett factor. Third, the inclusion--exclusion pair-density model
transfers directly to any pairwise counting problem with a singular
series.

\medskip
\noindent \textbf{Statistical rigor.} We are explicit about what is
\emph{not} claimed. In particular, the estimate of $c$ is sensitive to
the choice of window set, and we report this sensitivity rather than
suppressing it: the exclusion of $c = 1$ ranges from $2.6\sigma$ to
$5.6\sigma$ depending on the fit, whereas the exclusion of $c = 2$
ranges from $3.7\sigma$ to $14.4\sigma$ across window-set choices and
holds at $6.6\sigma$ on the bootstrap scale. We also decline to treat a
telescoping
identity involving the Chebyshev function as an independent
measurement; it is an internal consistency check of the sieve
pipeline only.

\medskip
\noindent \textbf{Reproducibility.} All code, CSV outputs, and a
README are released. The sieve, the windowed bootstrap, the
permutation test, and the correlogram are all seeded and deterministic.
Total computational cost is approximately 22~hours on a single
four-thread workstation with no GPU.
